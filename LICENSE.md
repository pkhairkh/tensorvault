### 4.3 Constituent Definitions

**4.3.1 "State"** means a sovereign political entity recognized as such under customary international law, including its Government, Government Agencies, and entities exercising governmental authority. The criteria for statehood are those set out in Article 1 of the Montevideo Convention on the Rights and Duties of States (1933).

**4.3.2 "Government"** means the executive, legislative, or judicial branch of a State at any level, including national, sub-national, regional, provincial, municipal, or local levels.

**4.3.3 "Government Agency"** means any entity that exercises governmental authority on behalf of a State or Government, including ministries, departments, bureaus, authorities, commissions, and state-owned enterprises exercising governmental functions.

**4.3.4 "Armed Force"** means the regular military forces of a State, including army, navy, air force, marine corps, space force, and reserve or national guard components, and any Paramilitary Organization formally integrated into the military command structure of a State.

**4.3.5 "Defense Contractor"** means any Legal Entity that:

(a) is registered under the U.S. International Traffic in Arms Regulations (22 CFR Part 122) as a manufacturer, exporter, or broker of defense articles; or

(b) is registered under an equivalent defense-articles registration regime of another jurisdiction; or

(c) derives more than ten percent (10%) of its annual gross revenue from contracts with an Armed Force, a defense ministry, or a Government Agency for the supply of Military Articles, Military Services, or Military Technology; or

(d) is Controlled by a Legal Entity described in (a), (b), or (c).

For purposes of this Section 4.3.5, **"Military Articles"** means articles, materials, or supplies specifically designed, developed, configured, adapted, or modified for military use, including without limitation items on the United States Munitions List (22 CFR Part 121) or equivalent lists of other jurisdictions. **"Military Services"** means services directly supporting military operations, military training, military logistics, or military command-and-control, including without limitation engineering, technical, consulting, or advisory services for military purposes. **"Military Technology"** means technology, including software and technical data, specifically designed, developed, configured, adapted, or modified for military use, or technology that is subject to export control as a defense article under ITAR (22 CFR Part 121) or equivalent regulations of other jurisdictions. Military Articles, Military Services, and Military Technology do not include general-purpose commercial goods, services, or technology (including without limitation office supplies, generic cloud-computing services, generic enterprise software, or commercial catering services) even when supplied to an Armed Force, defense ministry, or Government Agency.

**4.3.6 "Intelligence Agency"** means any Legal Entity whose primary function is the collection, analysis, production, or use of information for national security, foreign policy, intelligence, or counterintelligence purposes. Intelligence Agency includes any Legal Entity listed in Appendix A, Section I, and any Legal Entity Controlled by such an entity.

**4.3.7 "Law Enforcement Agency"** means any Legal Entity with general or specialized civil law-enforcement authority, including federal, state, provincial, municipal, and local police; border control and immigration enforcement; customs enforcement; and specialized enforcement agencies. Law Enforcement Agency excludes Intelligence Agencies (covered separately) and Armed Forces (covered separately).

**4.3.8 "Paramilitary Organization"** means any organization organized along military lines that is not part of the regular Armed Forces of a State, including gendarmerie, border guards, coast guards (where militarily organized), and irregular forces.

**4.3.9 "Prison Authority"** means any Legal Entity operating or administering prisons, jails, detention facilities, immigration detention centers, or correctional facilities, whether publicly or privately operated.

**4.3.10 "Military Subcontractor"** means any Legal Entity that supplies goods or services to a Defense Contractor for use in fulfilling a contract with an Armed Force, a defense ministry, or a Government Agency for military articles, military services, or military technology.

**4.3.11 "Government-Controlled Entity"** means any Legal Entity in which a State, Government, or Government Agency, directly or indirectly:

(a) holds more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity or equivalent governance interests; or

(b) has the power to appoint a majority of the governing body; or

(c) otherwise exercises Control.

**4.3.12 "Material Support"** means tangible or intangible support that significantly aids the recipient's activities, including without limitation:

(a) financial support exceeding the lesser of one hundred thousand United States dollars (USD 100,000) or one percent (1%) of the recipient's annual operating budget;

(b) provision of personnel, including seconded employees or contractors;

(c) provision of facilities, equipment, or technology;

(d) provision of training, expertise, or advisory services;

(e) provision of logistical or operational support.

**5.2.5 Torture.** Use in the commission of torture or other cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment, as defined in Article 1 of the Convention Against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment (1984).

**5.2.6 Slavery.** Use in the practice of slavery, the slave trade, or institutions and practices similar to slavery, as defined in the Slavery Convention (1926) and the Supplementary Convention on the Abolition of Slavery, the Slave Trade, and Institutions and Practices Similar to Slavery (1956).

**5.2.7 Forced Labor.** Use in the practice of forced or compulsory labor, as defined in Article 2 of ILO Convention No. 29 concerning Forced or Compulsory Labour (1930) and Article 1 of ILO Convention No. 105 concerning the Abolition of Forced Labour (1957).

**5.2.8 Child Labor.** Use in the practice of employing children in a manner that violates ILO Convention No. 138 concerning Minimum Age for Admission to Employment (1973) or ILO Convention No. 182 concerning the Prohibition and Immediate Action for the Elimination of the Worst Forms of Child Labour (1999).

**5.2.9 Human Trafficking.** Use in trafficking in persons, as defined in Article 3 of the Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children, supplementing the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (2000).

**5.2.10 Terrorism.** Use in the commission of an act intended to cause death or serious bodily injury to a civilian, or to any other person not taking an active part in the hostilities in a situation of armed conflict, when the purpose of such act, by its nature or context, is to intimidate a population, or to compel a Government or an international organization to do or to abstain from doing any act, as defined in Article 2 of the International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism (1999).

**5.2.11 Mass Surveillance.** Use for the systematic collection, processing, analysis, or retention of communications content or communications metadata, location data, biometric data, or other personally identifying data, of a population, without specific and individualized suspicion that the subjects of the surveillance have engaged, are engaging, or are about to engage in conduct prohibited by applicable criminal law, and without judicial or equivalent independent authorization particularized to each subject.

**5.2.12 Unlawful Biometric Surveillance.** Use for biometric surveillance, including facial recognition, in public spaces, in violation of applicable law, including the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5) and equivalent law of other jurisdictions.

**5.2.13 Autonomous Lethal Weapon System.** Use in the development, production, testing, deployment, or operation of a weapon system that, once activated, can select and engage targets without further human intervention.

**5.2.14 Chemical Weapon.** Use in the development, production, stockpiling, transfer, or use of chemical weapons, as defined in Article II of the Convention on the Prohibition of the Development, Production, Stockpiling and Use of Chemical Weapons and on Their Destruction (1993).

**5.2.15 Biological Weapon.** Use in the development, production, stockpiling, transfer, or use of biological weapons, as defined in Article I of the Convention on the Prohibition of the Development, Production and Stockpiling of Bacteriological (Biological) and Toxin Weapons and on Their Destruction (1972).

**5.2.16 Nuclear Weapon Program.** Use in the development, production, testing, or maintenance of nuclear weapons, in violation of the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (1968).

**5.2.17 Offensive Cyber Operation.** Use in the conduct of an offensive cyber operation, meaning the unauthorized access, deletion, modification, or disruption of information, information systems, or information networks, in violation of applicable criminal law.

**5.2.18 Election Manipulation.** Use in the manipulation of an election, including without limitation: unauthorized access to election infrastructure; dissemination of disinformation regarding the time, place, or manner of voting; suppression of lawful voter registration or turnout; or interference with the tabulation or certification of election results.

**5.2.19 Large-Scale Disinformation.** Use in the systematic production or dissemination of false or misleading information, at scale, where such information is intended to deceive the public on a matter of public interest, including without limitation matters of public health, public safety, or electoral processes.

**5.2.20 Unlawful Censorship.** Use in the implementation of censorship, in violation of Article 19 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966) and applicable law, including without limitation the systematic blocking, filtering, or removal of lawful expression, without due process.

**5.2.21 Environmental Crime.** Use in the commission of an act that constitutes a crime under applicable environmental law, including without limitation illegal mining, illegal logging, illegal wildlife trafficking, or illegal emission of pollutants.

**5.2.22 Organized Crime.** Use in the commission of an offense that constitutes participation in an organized criminal group, as defined in Article 5 of the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (2000).
